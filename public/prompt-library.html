Realism + taste laws

1 · Golden Rules

Apply these 8 guardrails to every generation flow to ensure premium output.

1

Photo first, animate second

Never go text → video. Always generate a clean high-fidelity still image first, then use image-to-video tools.

2

One muse, every time

Reuse the exact same model reference image (MUSE_REF) so it's always the same girl. This builds immediate brand recognition.

3

Camera language, not adjectives

Use precise camera specifications like "Hasselblad, 105mm, f/2.8, soft window light" instead of generic descriptors like "stunning, 4k, masterpiece".

4

Lock the garment

AI will try to redesign clothes. Always write: "do NOT change the garment — keep print, color, cut, fabric 100% identical" first in try-on prompts.

5

Generate 3–4, keep 1

Do not settle. Always inspect the generated face, hands, and fabric texture details before finalizing your selection.

6

Small motion in video

Stick to micro-movements (soft breathing, gentle breeze, a slow 45-degree turn). Fast or large motion causes warping and melting.

7

The light is the brand

If the frame lacks a warm champagne key light coupled with a single cool aqua edge glow, it does not look like Biolumin yet.

8

Restraint wins

If a composition feels cluttered, strip out elements. Large amounts of clean negative space read as premium luxury.

Video production rules

5 · Animate Stills → 5s Video

Turn your generated editorial stills into short, cinematic 5-second video clips using Kling.

Kling Motion Specs

Settings: Vertical 9:16 ratio, 5s duration, count = 3. Focus entirely on small, physics-accurate movement.

Avoid in all runs:

morphing, warping face, melting fabric, changing pattern, extra limbs, distorted hands, flicker, jitter, glitch

Cost-saving Tip: Test the motion physics on Kling's draft models first, then process only the perfect keepers in the high-fidelity final rendering step.
SHOT-BY-SHOT MOTION SPECIFICATIONS 
SHOT1 Hero     → Slow cinematic dolly-in, gentle fabric sway, subtle hair movement, soft breath, faint smile. Smooth, no morphing.
SHOT2 Movement → She turns 45°, sleeves flowing in slow-motion, camera holds. Real cloth physics.
SHOT3 Aqua     → Very slow push-in, teal+gold light shimmering, head tilts slowly to camera.
SHOT4 Macro    → Fingers gently shift fabric, focus breathing. Tiny micro-motion only.
SHOT5 Walk     → Smooth tracking as she walks away, hem swaying, cinematic gimbal feel.
SHOT6 End-card → Almost still, soft breath + slow warm smile, slight upward camera drift.
